1973 · THE FIRST HANDHELD CALL

A phone leaves the wall.

Motorola engineer Martin Cooper made a public call on a handheld cellular phone prototype in New York. It was heavy, short on battery, and remarkable for one reason: the call could travel with you.

A prototype made the call. A practical phone was still years away.

1983 · MOBILE GOES ON SALE

The handset becomes real.

The Motorola DynaTAC 8000X became the first handheld mobile phone approved for commercial sale in the United States. It was expensive and far from pocket-sized, but people could finally buy a mobile phone of their own.

The first commercial model followed a decade of engineering.

1994 · A COMPUTER IN YOUR HAND

The phone learns new tricks.

IBM Simon combined a phone with tools such as a calendar, contacts, notes, and email. Its touchscreen hinted at a new idea: a phone could be a small personal computer, not just a way to talk.

First shown in 1992; it went on sale in 1994.

2007 · TOUCH CHANGES THE SCREEN

Fingers become the interface.

The first iPhone put a phone, music player, and internet communicator behind a large multi-touch screen. Touch became the main way to move around, and the screen became the heart of the device.

Apple introduced the iPhone on January 9, 2007.
Tap. Swipe. Discover.04 / 07
2008 · APPS OPEN NEW DOORS

A phone becomes many things.

App stores and new mobile platforms let people add tools to their phones. The first Android phone arrived in 2008. A single device could now become a camera, a game console, a notebook, or something new with every download.

The T-Mobile G1 was the first Android-powered phone.

2010s · ALWAYS WITH YOU

The internet travels along.

Faster mobile networks made maps, music, video, and video calls feel close at hand. The smartphone moved from a special gadget into an everyday companion for billions of people.

Access still depends on networks, devices, and where people live.

2020s · THE PHONE KEEPS CHANGING

One screen is no longer the limit.

Foldable displays change the shape of the phone. On-device assistants add new ways to search and create. The next chapter is still being written, with better access, longer-lasting devices, and more useful connections still to solve.

New features vary by phone, network, country, and service.
